🔀Fed–Fasting Regulation

  • ΦFed: insulin ↑ → hepatic glycolysis (PFK-1) and glycogen synthesis ↑.
  • ΦFasting: glucagon ↑ → hepatic glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis ↑.
  • DLiver PFK-2/FBPase-2: glucagon → PKA phosphorylation → F2,6BP ↓ → PFK-1 ↓ and FBPase-1 released from inhibition.

🔋Cofactors and Energetics

  • Aminotransferases: PLP (B6). Glutamate dehydrogenase: NAD⁺ or NADP⁺ depending on direction.
  • DCPS I uses 2 ATP per carbamoyl phosphate; ASS1 uses ATP → AMP + PPi (2 high-energy bonds).
  • Urea cycle cost: 3 ATP molecules but 4 high-energy phosphate equivalents per urea.
  • Location: CPS I and OTC in the mitochondrial matrix; ASS1, ASL and arginase in the cytosol; ornithine/citrulline exchanged across the inner membrane.
  • Two nitrogens of urea: one from NH₄⁺ (via CPS I), one from aspartate (via ASS1) SUPPLEMENTARY.
  • ΦFed: amino acid use and protein synthesis ↑. Early fasting: muscle proteolysis supplies gluconeogenic substrate.
  • CProlonged starvation: ketone use lowers dependence on muscle protein.

🧭Hormonal Summary

  • ΦInsulin: anabolic — glycogen, lipid and protein synthesis.
  • ΦGlucagon: hepatic glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis and fasting adaptation.
  • ΦEpinephrine: rapid mobilization — liver/muscle glycogenolysis and adipose lipolysis.
  • ΦCortisol: longer-term gluconeogenic enzyme expression and protein substrate mobilization.
  • CAMPK: energy-stress sensor; switches off energy-consuming pathways.
  • PPARα: drives fatty acid oxidation and ketogenesis genes in fasting. SREBP-1c: drives lipogenic genes in the fed state.

⏳Must-Know Timing

Fed: 2–4 h absorptive→Early fast: glycogenolysis + gluconeogenesis→24–36 h: glycogen largely depleted→Days: ketones rising→Weeks: ketones supply much brain energy
  • Absorptive period ≈ 2–4 hours after eating, depending on meal composition.
  • Early fasting: liver glycogenolysis rises while gluconeogenesis is already active.
  • 24–36 hours: liver glycogen may be markedly depleted (timing varies).
  • CSeveral days: ketone production and cerebral ketone use progressively rise.
  • CMuscle protein sparing: lower brain glucose demand → less gluconeogenesis from amino acids.
  • PCritical exception: RBCs cannot use ketones (no mitochondria) — they always need glucose.

⚖️Clinical Distinctions

  • PStarvation ketosis vs DKA: starvation → lower glucose and milder acidosis; DKA → inadequate insulin action, often high glucose (can be euglycemic).
  • PKetotic vs hypoketotic hypoglycemia: inappropriately low ketones during fasting point to FAO defects or hyperinsulinism.
  • Physiological ketogenesis needs hepatic fatty acid delivery, mitochondrial β-oxidation and working ketogenic enzymes.
  • Cerebral adaptation: ketones reduce but never eliminate the brain’s glucose requirement.
  • PRefeeding syndrome: insulin surge → phosphate, K⁺, Mg²⁺ shift into cells → monitor electrolytes, fluids and clinical status in at-risk patients.
  • Hyperinsulinism clue: high insulin and C-peptide with low ketones and free fatty acids during hypoglycemia SUPPLEMENTARY.

🗂️Grouping Logic

  • DPGlucose-release defects (GSD I, FBPase, PC): hypoglycemia + lactic acidosis.

⚖️Classic Board Distractor Pairs — All Plates

🍽️ Digestion
  • SGLT1 = Na⁺-coupled glucose/galactose; GLUT5 = facilitated fructose; GLUT2 = basolateral exit.
  • Pancreatic lipase needs colipase (gut lumen); LPL needs ApoC-II (capillaries).
🍞 Carbohydrate
  • PFK-1 = glycolysis; FBPase-1 = gluconeogenesis.
  • Glycogen synthase = storage; glycogen phosphorylase = mobilization.
  • PDH = acetyl-CoA; pyruvate carboxylase = oxaloacetate.
  • Liver glycogen maintains blood glucose; muscle glycogen supplies local energy.
🧈 Lipid & Ketones
  • ACC makes malonyl-CoA; CPT I controls long-chain FA mitochondrial entry.
  • HMGCS2 = ketogenesis (mitochondrial); HMG-CoA reductase = cholesterol synthesis.
  • Liver makes ketones (no SCOT); peripheral tissues with mitochondria use them.
  • Acetyl-CoA gives no net glucose; glycerol does.
🥩 Protein & Nitrogen
  • CPS I = urea cycle (mitochondrial, NAG); CPS II = pyrimidine synthesis (cytosolic).
  • OTC deficiency = ↑ orotate; CPS I deficiency = normal/low orotate.
🎛️ Hormones
  • Insulin = fed-state storage; glucagon = hepatic fasting glucose output.
  • Glucagon acts on liver; epinephrine acts on liver and muscle.
⛽ Fed vs Fasting
  • Early fasting = glycogen; prolonged fasting = gluconeogenesis + ketogenesis.
  • Brain adapts to ketones; RBCs cannot.
📉 Clinical Patterns
  • Ketotic hypoglycemia = glucose supply problem; hypoketotic hypoglycemia = FAO defect or hyperinsulinism.
  • Starvation ketosis = low-normal glucose, mild acidosis; DKA = insulin deficiency, severe acidosis.
  • SCOT deficiency = ketones high (can’t use); HMG-CoA lyase deficiency = ketones low (can’t make).

🏁Final PLE Mental Checklist — All Plates

🍞 Carbohydrate
  • Fed: insulin ↑ → glycolysis, glycogenesis, lipogenesis, protein synthesis ↑.
  • Glycolysis: PFK-1; net 2 ATP + 2 NADH.
  • Gluconeogenesis: PC + PEPCK + FBPase-1 + G6Pase; 4 ATP + 2 GTP from pyruvate.
  • Glycogenesis: glycogen synthase. Glycogenolysis: glycogen phosphorylase.
🧈 Lipid & Ketones
  • Fatty acid synthesis: ACC, biotin/B7, NADPH.
  • Fatty acid oxidation: CPT I gates long-chain entry; malonyl-CoA inhibits.
  • Ketogenesis: HMGCS2 in liver mitochondria. Ketolysis: SCOT in extrahepatic mitochondria.
🥩 Protein & Nitrogen
  • Transaminases: PLP/B6; CPS I starts nitrogen disposal.
  • Urea cycle: 4 high-energy phosphate equivalents per urea.
  • Hyperammonemia + orotic acid: think OTC deficiency.
⛽ Fuel Yields
  • Glucose oxidation: 30–32 ATP. Palmitate: ≈106 ATP net.
  • Early fasting: glucagon ↑ → glycogenolysis + gluconeogenesis. Starvation: FA oxidation + ketogenesis → protein sparing.
📉 Clinical Patterns
  • Brain uses ketones in prolonged fasting; RBCs always need glucose.
  • Hypoketotic hypoglycemia: evaluate fatty acid oxidation and inappropriate insulin action.
🔎 Core Sequence
  • Blocked enzyme or transporter → accumulated substrate / depleted product → failed fuel adaptation → clinical presentation → confirmatory biochemical or molecular test.
